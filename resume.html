Elija Vida

PhD student, EPFL & Idiap Research Institute

Research

I work on spiking neural networks for sequence processing. This includes speech recognition on LibriSpeech and TIMIT with spiking architectures, the role of noise in spiking neuron models, dendritic computation, and spiking generative models for speech.

Projects

Spiking speech recognition

End-to-end speech recognition with spiking encoders in SpeechBrain, on TIMIT and LibriSpeech. Hybrid architectures that combine spiking and non-spiking recurrent layers, evaluated over multiple seeds against published baselines, with analyses of recurrence stability and energy cost. Results in preparation.

Noise as a resource in spiking neurons

How stochastic firing interacts with learning: noisy neuron models with the noise-driven learning rule, trainable noise scales, and the effect of noise schedules on accuracy and calibration on speech-command benchmarks. Includes an independent replication of the noisy spiking network of Ma et al. on CIFAR-10.

Dendritic processing units

Spiking networks with dendritic branches and heterogeneous, RRAM-realistic delays, applied to temporal benchmarks such as the Spiking Heidelberg Digits and biomedical time series. Started as my MSc thesis (code); now continued independently toward publication.

Spiking generative models

Diffusion and flow matching as a route to spiking speech synthesis, beginning with image generation as a controlled testbed. Early stage; the topic of my PhD candidacy project.
